Section 01

How to use this handbook

Read this section before your first session

TapBix helps you manage products, sales, purchases, inventory, customers, suppliers and accounts. It also supports multiple devices and independent branches on a local network.

Choose your starting point

Your roleStart with
Business ownerSetup, permissions, reports, financial management and backups.
CashierCustomers, checkout, returns, shifts and printing.
Purchasing officerSuppliers, purchases, purchase returns and consignment.
Warehouse keeperLocations, stock counts, transfers, receiving and inventory reports.

Before using live business data

  1. Check the application version, signed-in account, branch and current location.
  2. Practise with separate test data. Keep training transactions out of your live records.
  3. Take a backup before importing a large dataset or reconfiguring a device.
Menus can look different on a phone and a computer. This handbook follows section names and working steps, rather than fixed button positions or specific device names.

Section 02

Setting up your business

Welcome screen → Setup

  1. Choose the device function: standalone device, company master, independent branch server or connected device. To join an existing business, use an administrator’s invitation.
  2. Enter company information: name, contact details, currency and required settings. Check the currency before recording transactions.
  3. Set up the owner account: use private login credentials, then create a separate account for each employee.
  4. Review sales settings: taxes, payment methods, printing and stock policy.
  5. Add master data: categories, products and variants, suppliers and customers.
  6. Record stock: use a documented opening balance, purchase invoice or consignment receipt, according to its source.
  7. Verify: open the location balance and movement report to check your entries.
A simple start: a shop with one device can begin with its own products and sales. Add other devices and locations as the business needs them.
Renaming a company or device does not transfer stock or merge databases.

Company, branch, sales floor and warehouse

Branches, warehouses and devices

TermMeaning
CompanyThe business to which branches and locations belong.
BranchThe operating unit to which documents and accounts are attributed.
Branch sales floor / branch stockThe stock location within the branch. It may display the default location code.
Branch warehouseAn additional location belonging to the branch, with its own stock quantities.
Connected deviceA device working through its branch server, within its assigned location and permissions.

Choose the correct location

Before a purchase, sale or stock count, check the branch and stock location. Receive a purchase into the place where the goods actually arrive. Use a transfer if they move later.

The sales floor holds 10 units and the warehouse holds 20. The combined balance is 30 units. Transferring and receiving 5 units from the warehouse into the sales floor leaves 15 units in each.
Creating a warehouse or connecting a device adds no stock. The supplier account belongs to the branch ledger; the receiving location determines where the quantity is held.

Navigation, search and document status

Dashboard and menus

  1. Open a section from the dashboard or navigation menu. Phones may use cards where computers use a sidebar.
  2. Search by document number, name or barcode, as supported by the screen.
  3. Check filters before concluding that data is missing: date, branch, warehouse, customer or supplier, and status.
  4. Open a document card to inspect its lines, payments and status, then use its available actions.
  5. After changing a filter, wait for loading to finish. Refresh when you need the latest reading.
StatusMeaning
DraftThe transaction is incomplete. Review it before saving or posting.
PostedThe document’s effects have been recorded according to its type.
Cancelled / reversedA cancellation or reversal was recorded. Open its history to see why.
In transitA transfer has been dispatched and has not yet been fully received.
Available actions depend on account permissions, device function and document status. Check these before creating a replacement transaction.

Section 05

Creating products and categories

Products → Add product

  1. Create clear categories, such as “Drinks” or “Clothing”, then open Add product.
  2. Enter a recognisable product name, category and required identification details.
  3. Enter its product code and barcode where applicable. Use a unique identity for each distinct product.
  4. Select the unit of measure, tracking options and variants appropriate to the product.
  5. Review selling prices, cost, limits and any other fields shown in the form.
  6. Save, then check that searches by name, code and barcode find the product.

Editing or deactivating a product

Open the product and choose Edit. Preserve its identity when it has transaction history; changing a name does not create a new product. Use the available deactivate or archive action for products you no longer use.

Import and export

Use the import template provided by the application. Review column mapping, previews and errors before confirming. Start with a small file, then export the product list for review or storage.

A product record defines an item. Enter quantities through the appropriate inventory documents and avoid repeating stock already recorded.

Section 06

Variants, weight, length and volume

Products → Variants / Unit settings

Products with colours or sizes

  1. Create the base product, such as “Shirt”.
  2. Add its available attributes, such as colour and size.
  3. Create each actual variant, such as “Blue / Medium”, with its code, barcode and price as needed.
  4. Select the specific variant on invoices and transfers.

Products sold in fractional quantities

  1. Select the unit used by the price, such as kilogram, metre or litre.
  2. Enter the quantity in that unit. At a price per kilogram, 0.5 means half a kilogram.
  3. Review pack-to-base-unit conversions when using packaging units.
  4. Check the quantity and total before saving or returning goods.
At 40 per metre, a sale of 2.5 metres has a line value of 100 before discount and tax. Enter 2.5 when the selected unit is metres.
Use matching variants and units across purchases, sales, transfers and returns. Check batch and expiry details for products that require tracking.

Section 07

Prices, discounts and taxes

Business settings and invoice screens

Before the first invoice

Review the currency and tax policy, including whether prices include tax or have tax added. Choose the setting used by your business and check the invoice preview.

  1. Check each line’s unit price and quantity.
  2. Select the available discount mode: per item or per invoice.
  3. Enter a fixed amount or a percentage as the field requires.
  4. Review promotional discounts before adding a manual discount.
  5. Check subtotal, discount, tax, total, paid amount and outstanding balance.
  6. Save when the final amount matches the agreement with the customer or supplier.
A line worth 100 with a 10% discount becomes 90 after discount. The final tax amount depends on the document’s tax and price-inclusion settings.
A linked return uses the original invoice. Do not substitute today’s price or tax settings for the values of an earlier transaction.

Section 08

Suppliers and supplier accounts

Suppliers → Add supplier / Supplier details

  1. Search for the supplier before creating a new record.
  2. Enter the name, contact information and required details.
  3. If using an opening balance, enter only a documented amount that predates your use of the system.
  4. Assign each purchase invoice and purchase return to the correct supplier.
  5. Record actual payments using the appropriate payment action, then review the supplier statement.

Reading a statement

Select the period and branch. Review purchases, returns, payments and adjustments by type. Open the document behind a movement to understand the amount.

Suppliers and branch warehouses

A purchase delivered to a branch warehouse belongs to that branch’s ledger, with the warehouse identified as the receiving location. Use the existing supplier record for other locations of the same branch.

Moving goods between company locations does not create another purchase. Supplier identity may remain attached to the goods for source tracking without creating a new liability just for the move.

Section 09

Creating a purchase invoice

Purchases → New invoice

  1. Supplier and location: select the supplier and check the branch and actual receiving location.
  2. Document details: check the date, invoice number and available reference. Use the due date for an outstanding amount payable later.
  3. Lines: search or scan, then select variant, unit, quantity and unit cost.
  4. Tracking: enter batch and expiry details where required.
  5. Calculation: review line discounts, invoice discount, tax and available charges.
  6. Payment: enter the amount paid and method. Check the supplier balance for credit or partly paid purchases.
  7. Confirm: save, then reopen the saved invoice to check its lines and location.

After saving

Check the increased location stock, supplier movement and payment. Print or share the document using the available actions.

When recorded goods move from the receiving area to the sales floor, use a transfer. Avoid entering the purchase again.

Section 10

Purchase returns and adjustments

Purchases → Purchase returns

When the original invoice is available

  1. Open the invoice or start a return linked to it.
  2. Select the lines and quantities actually being returned. Check the remaining returnable quantity.
  3. Verify the issuing stock location, variant and batch where applicable.
  4. Enter the return reason and review the line value, discount and tax being reversed.
  5. Select the available financial treatment: a refund received or a supplier balance adjustment, according to the agreement and actual payment.
  6. Confirm, then review inventory, the supplier statement and the resulting document.

When no original invoice can be linked

Use an adjustment return only when your permissions allow it. Enter the required source, value and reason. Review this independent document carefully; it does not automatically prove a connection to an earlier invoice.

A return from a branch warehouse affects stock at that location and the supplier account in the branch ledger. Check the direction of money: receiving a supplier refund differs from paying a refund to a customer.

Customers, balances and loyalty points

Customers → Customer details

  1. Search by name or phone before adding a customer.
  2. Enter customer details and check any documented opening balance.
  3. Select the customer on the sale so credit, payments and points are attributed correctly.
  4. Open the statement to review sales, returns, collections and the outstanding amount.

Using points to pay

  1. Select the customer before completing the sale.
  2. At checkout, review the available points and their value under your loyalty policy.
  3. Enter the points to redeem within the permitted limit, then check the remaining payment and its method.
  4. After saving, review the customer balance and points in the transaction or customer account.
A connected device relies on its branch server to confirm the balance. If verification fails, restore the connection or agree on another payment method with the customer.
For credit or partly paid sales, review the customer balance after posting. The invoice total may differ from the money collected.

Section 12

Creating a sale and completing checkout

Sales → New sale

  1. Check the location: verify the branch and sales floor or warehouse issuing the stock.
  2. Add products: search or scan, then select variant, quantity and unit.
  3. Select the customer: this matters for credit, points and statements. Select a salesperson when tracking sales and commissions.
  4. Review pricing: check discounts, promotions and tax on each line and the invoice.
  5. Check stock sources: if an owned/consignment or supplier allocation is shown, verify the available source.
  6. Open checkout: select a payment method and enter money received and any points redeemed.
  7. Confirm once: wait for the result, then open the saved invoice to print or share it.

Before handing over the goods

Check the invoice number, total, paid amount and remaining balance. Confirm that posting has completed.

If the connection fails after saving, check the invoice list and transaction status before repeating the sale. A connected device needs access to its branch server.

Section 13

Sales returns and cancellation

Sales → Returns / Invoice details

  1. Open the original invoice and choose the return action.
  2. Select products and quantities within the remaining returnable amount.
  3. Record the reason and actual disposition of the goods: return to stock or another available treatment appropriate to their condition.
  4. Review refund amount and method. Choose cash, card, account credit or cheque according to available options and the actual transaction.
  5. For unsaleable consignment goods, review whether the supplier or business bears responsibility and record the reason.
  6. Confirm, then check the customer balance, stock, points and return document.

Returns and cancellation

A return handles goods brought back after a sale. Cancellation or reversal corrects a document according to its status and your permissions while retaining a record of the action. Read the action before confirming.

Repeating a return does not increase the returnable quantity. If an invoice from another branch cannot be verified, wait for verification or use an authorised adjustment procedure with a documented reason.

Section 14

Promotions and promotional discounts

Promotions and discounts

  1. Add a promotion and enter a name that explains its purpose and duration.
  2. Select a promotion type available in your version.
  3. Select eligible products, categories or conditions.
  4. Enter the required discount, quantity or reward terms for that type.
  5. Review start and end dates, active status and any limits shown in the form.
  6. Save, then test both an eligible and an ineligible basket using training data.

During a sale

Review the discount applied before adding a manual discount. Check how changes to quantities, products or the customer affect eligibility.

When a promotion ends

Check its status and dates before changing base prices. A saved document retains its transaction values; an expired promotion does not require editing old invoices.

If a promotion is missing, first check activation, dates, eligible products, quantities and conditions. Understand the cause before substituting a manual discount.

Section 15

Stock balances and movements

Products and inventory reports

Understanding a balance

Select the location, product, variant and unit. Review available, owned, consignment, reserved and in-transit quantities where shown.

  1. Open the product balance at the required location.
  2. Review recent purchases, sales, returns, transfers and stock counts.
  3. Open the source document for any movement that needs explanation.
  4. Use valuation reports for stock value and movement reports for quantities and reasons.

Opening stock

Use a documented opening balance for goods held before recording began. Check quantity, unit cost, ownership and location. Move goods already recorded elsewhere using a transfer.

Stock quantity and stock value answer different questions. Consignment quantities may belong to a supplier and should not automatically be treated as business-owned assets.
A sales-floor balance of 10 and a warehouse balance of zero will give different results when those locations are selected on a sale or report.

Section 16

Stock counts, adjustments and batches

Inventory → Count or adjustment / Expiry reports

  1. Select the branch and location before starting a count.
  2. Select products and variants to count, checking their units.
  3. Enter physically counted quantities and review differences from recorded balances.
  4. Choose the shortage or surplus reason and review required source, ownership and cost details.
  5. Confirm after review, then open the product movement and resulting document.

Batches and expiry

For batch-tracked items, record the batch and expiry on receipt. Review near-expiry and expired-stock reports. Select the correct batch when a transaction requests it.

Explain differences before adjusting

If a difference comes from an incomplete invoice or transfer, resolve that document first. Keep pending movements and stock held at other locations in mind during the count.

Keep the count record and reason. Changing today’s product cost does not automatically correct an earlier movement; use the appropriate procedure for that document.

Transferring between sales floors and warehouses

Branches, warehouses and devices → Transfers

Source dispatchesGoods in transitDestination receives
  1. Select the source location and destination location. Read the branch name, location type and code, especially for similar names.
  2. Search products and select variants and quantities.
  3. Review owned and consignment quantities and supplier/source allocation where available.
  4. Choose review and save, then check the summary before dispatch.
  5. After dispatch the goods are in transit. Contact the receiving operator if follow-up is needed.

Wrong destination?

Open the outgoing transfer and use “Recall remaining” when available. Check the destination’s status and quantities already received. If receiving has completed, use an appropriate documented return transfer.

An internal transfer does not create a customer sale or a new supplier purchase. Keep one documented stock movement for the transfer.

TapBixInventory
Section 18

Receiving transfers and tracking progress

Transfers → In progress → Incoming transfer

  1. Use the receiving location’s device with an account permitted to receive.
  2. Open the incoming transfer and check source, destination and lines.
  3. Match the physical goods against the document. Enter quantities received in good condition and any damaged or missing quantities and reasons requested by the form.
  4. Confirm receipt and wait for confirmation.
  5. Check destination stock and transfer status. With partial receiving, the remainder stays open for follow-up.
  6. At the source, verify that the receipt and updated status have arrived.

If a transfer is missing

Check connection to the branch server, device and user location assignments, synchronisation and filters, then refresh. Network isolation can prevent communication even when devices appear to use the same network.

Notifications draw attention to a transfer. The saved document and transfer list provide its recorded status. Verify the original operation before entering an alternative receipt.

Section 19

Consignment: agreements and receiving

Consignment management

Consignment goods remain supplier-owned until the treatment specified in the agreement. A product may have both owned and consignment quantities.

  1. Open consignment agreements and create one for the correct supplier.
  2. Set the supplier entitlement method and available terms, then review covered products and locations.
  3. Record a consignment receipt for goods actually received, specifying location, variant and required source.
  4. After confirming, review custody quantities and the supplier report.

Loading items from an invoice

Populating lines from a supplier invoice saves data entry. Review the action’s effect before confirming; check whether the quantity has already been recorded.

Changing ownership of existing stock

Use the available conversion of existing stock to consignment when its conditions apply. A new receipt would add more quantity.

Keep the agreement reference. A product name containing “consignment” does not define ownership; the stock source and agreement do.

Section 20

Consignment: sales, returns and settlement

Consignment management → Agreement and reports

  1. During a sale, review owned/consignment allocation and supplier terms.
  2. Review consignment sales and entitlements before creating a settlement.
  3. Create the settlement for the required period or movements using the options provided.
  4. Check the amount due to the supplier, then record the actual payment and method.
  5. Open reconciliation to check stock, custody, settlements and payments.

Returning custody stock and recording damage

Use a custody return document for goods sent back to the supplier. For damaged or missing stock, record quantity, reason and responsibility under the agreement using the available action.

Customer returns

Link to the original sale when available. Review the quantity’s return to its source and the adjustment to supplier entitlement, particularly if the sale was already settled.

Check the agreement and settlement before creating a purchase or supplier payment to resolve a consignment issue. Use the document that represents the actual event.

Expenses, collections and payments

Expenses and customer/supplier details

Recording an expense

  1. Add an expense and choose its date, category or account.
  2. Enter amount, a clear description and supporting document reference where available.
  3. Select payment method or the required cash/bank account.
  4. Review, save and check the expense report.

Collecting from a customer or paying a supplier

  1. Open the correct account and review the amount due.
  2. Choose payment or collection and enter amount, date and method.
  3. Check invoice allocation where the action supports it.
  4. Save, then check the statement and cash or bank balance.
A customer pays 200 against a 500 invoice, leaving 300 due. When the remaining 300 arrives, record a collection against the account.
A payment already included in an invoice should not be recorded again as a separate expense or collection for the same event.

Section 22

Cheques and due dates

Financial management → Cheque management

  1. Select an incoming or outgoing cheque according to its direction.
  2. Enter customer or supplier, amount, cheque number, bank, issue date and due date as required.
  3. Review status and save.
  4. Follow the cheque through actual clearing or payment, then use the appropriate status action.
  5. For a bounced or cancelled cheque, use the designated action and record the reason.

Daily follow-up

Review due and overdue cheques and any partial payments. Match cleared receipts and payments to actual bank movements.

Issuing or receiving a cheque does not always move the bank balance immediately. Check its status and journal entry before adding any further cash movement.

Purchase due dates

Due dates help track amounts payable later to suppliers. Enter the agreed date and review outstanding payments. A due date alone does not mean an invoice has been paid.

Section 25

Reconciliation and investigating differences

Financial management → Reconciliation and health

  1. Run the checks and read each result.
  2. If inventory and ledger values differ, identify the location, period and amounts involved.
  3. Review related opening balances, purchases, returns, transfers and adjustments.
  4. Open the source documents and check their completion and status.
  5. Take a backup before any treatment that changes the books.
  6. After a documented correction, repeat the checks and keep the report.

Reporting a problem

Prepare the application version, device type and function, branch, location, document number and steps leading to the message. Hide sensitive data before sharing a screenshot.

Equal debits and credits alone do not prove inventory reconciliation. Identify the source of a difference before posting a correction.

Employees / Salespeople and commission reports

Cashier shifts and end-of-day review

Cashier shifts

  1. Open the shift using the responsible account and enter the required opening cash.
  2. Perform sales, returns and payments using the correct account.
  3. Record additional cash movements through the available action with a clear reason.
  4. At closing, count actual cash and review other payment methods.

End-of-day checks

  • Unconfirmed invoices and unfinished drafts.
  • The day’s returns and payments.
  • Dispatched transfers awaiting receipt.
  • Branch synchronisation and backup status.
Closing a shift concerns cashier activity. Closing an accounting period is a separate action after financial review.

Connecting devices within a branch

Devices and network / Device setup before login

  1. Choose the device holding the branch database. Keep it available while staff work.
  2. Start the company master or branch-server service as configured.
  3. In device and invitation management, select the new device’s function and location.

Section 30

Creating an independent branch and warehouses

Branches, warehouses and devices → Company branches

  1. On the administration device, create the branch with a clear name and code.
  2. Review its default stock location. Add a warehouse for a separate physical stock location.
  3. Set the branch assortment: all company products or a managed selection suited to its activity.
  4. Create an independent branch-server invitation and enter it on the intended device.
  5. Verify setup completion, branch, locations and catalogue before recording transactions.
  6. Connect staff devices to that branch server and assign locations and permissions.
  7. Record opening stock using purchases, consignment receipts or documented transfers according to source.

Branches with different activities

A clothing branch may need a different assortment from a pharmacy. Use managed assortments, categories and appropriate exceptions while preserving product identities.

Creating a branch does not copy the main branch’s stock into it. The company master handles central administration; the branch server runs its own branch.

Section 31

Monitoring branches and connection status

Branches, warehouses and devices → Branch monitoring centre

  1. Open the centre from the administration device with an authorised account.
  2. Check connected branches and last synchronisation before relying on totals.

Backups, restore and updates

Settings → Backup and restore

Creating a backup

  1. Identify the device holding the branch database. Back up that server’s data.
  2. Open backup and choose create and save.
  3. Wait for confirmation and keep the file in another trusted location accessible if the device fails.
  4. Record branch and backup date, and keep anything required to open the protected backup.

Restoring

  1. Back up the current state before restoring.
  2. Choose the correct branch file and read the operation’s effect before confirming.
  3. Follow the restore or branch-server replacement workflow where networking is involved.
  4. Verify products, documents, accounts and synchronisation afterwards.

Before updating

Take a backup, resolve uncertain transactions and update connected devices to compatible versions. Allow time to verify operation afterwards.

Run one independent server for each branch database. Before restoring an older backup, review any newer data it would replace.

Daily checklist and worked example

Use this section for staff training

Start of day

  • Check branch-server availability, connection, account and current location.
  • Open a shift if required and review stock and due-date alerts.

One complete stock cycle

  1. Purchase 20 units into the branch warehouse, recording supplier, cost and payment method.
  2. Transfer and receive 5 units into the sales floor. Warehouse stock becomes 15 and sales-floor stock 5.
  3. Sell 2 units from the sales floor to the correct customer. Sales-floor stock becomes 3.
  4. If the customer returns 1 saleable unit, create a linked sales return with return to stock. Sales-floor stock becomes 4.
  5. Review product movements, supplier and customer statements, and sales and return reports.

End of day

  • Review pending transactions, transfers, returns and payments.
  • Reconcile cash and the shift, then check the last synchronisation.
  • Create and store a backup in the approved location.
Run this exercise with training data and keep unrelated factors disabled. Add taxes, discounts and consignment as separate training cases later.
